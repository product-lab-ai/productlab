# Recovery result

Draft recovery is ready for founder review as a local prototype. It restored the
unfinished step and actual program in both declared development tasks. A fresh
prediction and execution remained required. This establishes functional recovery,
not learner benefit or acceptance.

Open `generated/workings-product-review-1/index.html` from the shared project
folder. The original Workings checkout is unchanged. The review includes three
separate standalone builds and links to their evidence.

## Verification and limits

- Workings: 216 tests passed, including six focused recovery tests. Its captured
  baseline had 210 tests. The candidate passed the production build and the
  existing browser sweep through both agent lessons.
- Product Lab: 38 tests passed, including four capture tests for local edits,
  unsafe destinations, symlinks, and changes during copying. Ruff, formatting,
  and the skill validator passed.
- Focused browser review: 22 checks passed across both alternatives. Keyboard
  navigation reached the edit on a narrow screen. The inspected states had no axe
  violations. Blocked storage left the activities usable with clear notices.
- All 155 original source files and the captured baseline still match the
  manifest. Applying `candidate.patch` to a fresh copy reproduced its five changed
  files exactly. The earlier comparison trial remains separate.

The completed evaluator calls took 264.57 seconds in total. Monetary cost was not
reported for any call. Model, authoring, manual review, and total learning costs
are unknown. The report's numeric zero for reported cost does not mean free.

Two initial observation runs failed because the harness mishandled the coin screen
and same-route navigation. The first focused review resized an already focused
control and incorrectly expected a second focus call to scroll it into view.
The corrected review traversed from the lesson with Tab and passed. Those failed
records remain alongside the successful runs. No scored call was replaced.
An initial candidate type check also caught an overly narrow generic parameter;
it was fixed before the successful 216-test run. No product result is inferred
from these harness and authoring failures.

The prototypes cover guided agent lessons in local Chromium. They do not save
free-play drafts, define conflicts between tabs, cover all subjects, or sync
across devices. Exact content is stored as the recovery binding, so larger lesson
sets may need a smaller representation. Installed dependencies were shared, not
independently archived. No production release or security certification is claimed.

## Retained lesson and next decision

The study retains `separate-unfinished-work` as **proposed**: preserve current work
separately from historical results, bind it to content and engine, validate it,
and require fresh execution. It includes a counterexample: deliberately resetting
an activity might support practice better than restoring every edit.

Reserved validation is unused. The existing browser sweep covers the retry lesson,
and its content was already known. That reserved task must not be called a blind
or fresh test of general reuse. A transfer claim needs a new task and a comparison
with ordinary assistant work and simple retrieval of prior decisions.

The next product decision is whether draft recovery fits the intended learning
experience. Use founder review and the existing learner pilot to examine effort,
preference, and explanation. Pilot evidence export is the next practical
opportunity; learner-selected diagnostic experiments remain an ambitious untested
hypothesis. The earlier attempt-comparison mechanisms remain available separately.
